## A useful detour: where the problem comes from

Medical computing was born as an **administrative and legal** tool: meeting regulatory obligations,
not supporting care. Uses were added by organic growth, one need after another — billing, bed
management, operating theatre, imaging, laboratory, emergency — each with its own software, scope and
interfaces.

The result: an information system drifting into spaghetti architecture, data produced *after the
fact* for reporting rather than for action, and professionals manually patching the digital gaps.

<Callout variant="warning">
The hospital information system as a **system of systems** remains largely unexamined. That
conceptual gap, more than technology, explains the current state.
</Callout>

Digital technology moved into a new era without the hospital: web 2.0 and open platforms, the
smartphone and IoT explosion, big data and AI everywhere else. Hospitals chose homogenisation over
standardisation. Covid made the bill visible: laborious exchanges, spreadsheets, re-keying, and no
reliable consolidated view of beds, tests or patient flows.

FHIR was the first massive worldwide answer — the Covid vaccination campaign was its full-scale test.

### Reading a FHIR interaction

```http
GET /Appointment?date=ge2026-09-18&date=le2026-09-19&status=booked
     &_include=Appointment:location
     &_count=200
Accept: application/fhir+json
```

```json
{
  "resourceType": "Appointment",
  "id": "a-10432",
  "status": "booked",
  "serviceType": [{
    "coding": [{ "system": "http://snomed.info/sct", "code": "394914008", "display": "Radiology" }]
  }],
  "start": "2026-09-18T09:30:00+02:00",
  "end": "2026-09-18T09:50:00+02:00",
  "participant": [
    { "actor": { "reference": "Patient/8821" }, "status": "accepted" },
    { "actor": { "reference": "Location/mri-room-2" }, "status": "accepted" }
  ]
}
```

<Reveal question="Three things are still missing before you can tell whether Argus can use this resource. Which?">
1. **Which profile** constrains it? Are `status`, `serviceType` and `participant` mandatory in your
   context? (Profiling module)
2. **Which ValueSet** binds `serviceType`, and with what binding strength? Without that, every silo
   sends its own list. (Semantics module)
3. **Which provenance**: who produced this instance, when, from which source, and who corrects it if
   it is wrong? (Architecture module)
</Reveal>

## EHDS: interoperability becomes an obligation

The European Health Data Space is a structuring regulation with a very wide scope: all medical and
medico-administrative software, all healthcare organisations, all actors in the care chain — and the
citizen.

  columns={3}

EHDS moves the goalposts: interoperability stops being an improvement project and becomes a
**condition of market access**.
</Callout>

## Strengths and fragilities — reading a national ecosystem

The French case is instructive because it is typical: adoption blocked not by technology, but by an
economic vicious circle — then unblocked from the field.

## Takeaway

FHIR is not "one more format". It is a **platform standard**: a shared data model, a uniform API, a
family of paradigms, and an ecosystem of tools — servers, validators, SDKs, implementation guides.

But a generic standard still leaves far too much freedom to guarantee real interoperability. The next
module covers that missing layer: **profiling**.
